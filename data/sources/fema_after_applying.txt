I Applied for Assistance. What's Next?
Source: FEMA.gov, https://www.fema.gov/assistance/individual/after-applying (last updated June 26, 2026). U.S. government work.

FEMA provides money for the basic repairs to make a home safe, sanitary and livable. Your assistance is determined by comparing your recorded essential losses and serious needs to the types of assistance available within the FEMA Individual Assistance program. You may also be referred to the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) for low-interest disaster loans.

Stay in Touch

You can sign up for updates by email, phone calls, and text message. Official FEMA updates come from:
- Emails from noreply-ecorr@dhs.gov, fema-automessaging@fema.intouchconnections.com or fema-automessaging@fema.dhs.gov.
- Calls from 1-800-621-3362 or 1-866-863-8673. Sometimes FEMA calls with a recorded voice message about your application.
- Texts from 43362 or 91908.

Protect yourself against scams. If you receive suspicious emails or phone calls, call the FEMA Helpline at 1-800-621-3362 to check whether a FEMA call or email is real.

Read any messages FEMA sends you carefully. They explain FEMA's decisions and the steps you need to take. If your address or phone number changes since you applied, update it right away by logging into DisasterAssistance.gov or calling 1-800-621-3362.

Check Your Application Status

Your application status may change a few times during review. You can check the latest status by logging into DisasterAssistance.gov, selecting your application, and selecting the Status tab.

Making a Correction or Filing an Appeal

Call 1-800-621-3362 or visit a Disaster Recovery Center (DRC) to make corrections to your application. You can upload documents to your application online.

If your status says "has not been approved," this can be for a variety of reasons. Check online for a copy of your letter that explains the specific reasons. Many times FEMA just needs some extra information, or you may need to correct information. If you disagree with FEMA's decision, you can appeal.

Verify Your Identity

To receive assistance you need to prove your identity with a valid Social Security number. FEMA usually checks your identity using public records when you apply. If it cannot, FEMA may ask for more information.

If you applied for yourself or your household, accepted documents include:
- Documentation from the Social Security Administration, or another federal entity, with the full or last four digits of your SSN
- Social Security card if sent with federal or state-issued identification
- Employer's payroll document with the full or last four digits of your SSN
- Military identification
- Marriage license to confirm proof of maiden name
- U.S. passport

If you applied on behalf of a U.S. citizen minor child:
- Any of the documents above in the child's name, OR
- The child's birth certificate AND a copy of the child's Social Security card or documentation from the Social Security Administration, or another federal entity, with the full or last four digits of the child's SSN.

Home Inspection

After you apply, FEMA may need to verify your home and personal property damage through an on-site or remote inspection. Inspectors identify damage caused by the disaster, but they do not decide whether you will receive assistance. If your home cannot be reached, the inspector may meet you at the obstruction or a neutral location. Do not wait for the inspection to start cleaning up, if it is safe. Make a list of your losses, take photos of the damage and keep all receipts.

Submit Documentation

Send documents with your name and application number by:
- Uploading them in the Upload Center at DisasterAssistance.gov.
- Mailing copies (keep the originals) to FEMA, P.O. Box 10055, Hyattsville, MD 20782-8055.
- Faxing to 1-800-827-8112.
You can also visit a Disaster Recovery and Document Drop-off Center to have documents scanned into your file.

Getting Help From a Friend or Relative

You can have a friend or relative talk to FEMA for you by submitting a third-party statement. This helps if you are displaced and cannot be at the home inspection, or if you need help with the application, for example senior citizens, people who have difficulty understanding spoken or written information, and people with mental, physical or sensory challenges.

To let FEMA share your information with a third party, complete FEMA Form FF-104-FY-21-118, Authorization for the Release of Information Under the Privacy Act. It allows another person over 18 to give and receive information from FEMA on your behalf. Upload it to DisasterAssistance.gov, mail it to FEMA National Processing Service Center, P.O. Box 10055, Hyattsville, MD 20782-8055, fax it to 1-800-827-8112, or bring it to a Disaster Recovery Center. Verbal permission is only accepted when both you and the helper are at a Disaster Recovery Center or on the phone together with the FEMA Helpline.
