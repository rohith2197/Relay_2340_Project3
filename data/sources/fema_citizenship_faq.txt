FEMA FAQ: Citizenship Status and Eligibility for Disaster Assistance (includes information sharing)
Source: FEMA.gov, https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20250602/faq-citizenship-status-and-eligibility-disaster-assistance. FAQ first released December 1, 2018 (DR-4407-CA); page last updated March 18, 2021. U.S. government work. Some details are older than FEMA's current citizenship page.

1. What help is there for people who are not U.S. citizens?
If you are not a U.S. Citizen, Non-Citizen National or Qualified Alien, you may still be eligible if you are the parent or guardian of a minor child who lives with you and who is a U.S. Citizen, Non-Citizen National, or Qualified Alien. You can register using the child's Social Security number. If the child does not have a Social Security number, you can still apply by getting the child a Social Security number and calling FEMA to register within 60 days of the disaster declaration date. For questions about your right to apply, or help finding volunteer agencies, call (800) 621-3362.

2. What are FEMA's citizenship/immigration requirements?
You must be a U.S. Citizen, Non-Citizen National, or Qualified Alien to be eligible for FEMA cash assistance programs (Individuals and Households Program and Disaster Unemployment Assistance). You do not need to be a citizen or qualified alien for crisis counseling, disaster legal services or other short-term, non-cash emergency assistance.

3. If I am an undocumented immigrant, am I eligible for assistance for disaster-related needs?
Yes, you may be eligible under many programs run by state and local agencies and voluntary agencies.

4. If I am an undocumented immigrant, am I eligible for any assistance from FEMA?
You may be eligible for short-term, non-cash emergency aid from FEMA. You are not personally eligible for FEMA cash assistance; however, you may apply on behalf of your U.S. citizen child, or another adult household member may qualify the household.

5. If I am an undocumented immigrant, can I apply on behalf of my child who was born in the United States?
Yes, you can apply on behalf of your minor child (under 18) if you live together. You will not have to provide any information on your immigration status or sign any documents regarding your status.

6. Do I need a Social Security number to register for FEMA cash assistance?
If you are applying on behalf of your minor child, you should provide the child's Social Security number.

7. Will my registration information be shared with immigration or other law enforcement agencies?
FEMA will not proactively provide applicant information to immigration or law enforcement organizations. However, in rare circumstances, based on a specific request, a FEMA applicant's personal information may be shared within the Department of Homeland Security, which includes FEMA, Customs and Border Protection, Citizenship and Immigration Services, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the Transportation Security Administration, the U.S. Coast Guard, the U.S. Secret Service and the Federal Law Enforcement Training Center.

8. Are foreign students eligible for FEMA cash assistance?
Foreign students may get non-cash, in-kind relief (search and rescue, food, water, shelter, emergency medical care) but not cash assistance.

9. If I have a Social Security number, am I a "Qualified Alien"?
Not necessarily. Having a Social Security number does not automatically make you a Qualified Alien.

Types of federal disaster assistance available regardless of immigration status: emergency assistance (such as sheltering), disaster legal services, crisis counseling, disaster case management, and disaster food assistance.
