Disaster Fraud: how to spot scams and real FEMA contacts
Source: FEMA.gov, https://www.fema.gov/assistance/individual/disaster-fraud (last updated June 26, 2026). U.S. government work.

After a disaster, scam artists, identity thieves and other criminals often try to take advantage of disaster survivors. Watch for and report any suspicious activity.

FEMA Housing Inspectors

A real FEMA inspector will:
- Never ask for your nine-digit registration number. They already have it in their records.
- Always wear an official government badge. Ask the person to show you their identification.
- Not ask for bank information. FEMA never charges a fee for an inspection.
- Not climb on roofs or enter crawl spaces.

Fake Offers of Local or Federal Aid
- Don't trust someone who asks for money. DHS, FEMA, SBA, and other federal agencies will never charge applicants for disaster assistance or for help filling out applications.
- Don't believe anyone who promises a disaster grant and asks for large cash deposits or advance payments in full.

Fraudulent Building Contractors
- Use licensed or verified local contractors backed by reliable references.
- Don't pay more than half the cost of repairs in advance.
- Ask contractors to describe the job in writing, with guarantees.

Identity Theft
Criminals may try to apply for FEMA assistance using names, addresses and Social Security numbers stolen from survivors.
- If a FEMA inspector comes to your home and you have not applied, your information may have been used without your knowledge. Tell the inspector you did not apply.
- If you get a letter from FEMA but did not apply, call the FEMA Helpline at 800-621-3362 (7 a.m. to 11 p.m. ET daily).
- Visit IdentityTheft.gov for steps to take if you think your identity was stolen.

Report Fraud or Scams
- FEMA Investigations and Inspections Division: email StopFEMAFraud@fema.dhs.gov, fax 202-212-4926, or mail 400 C Street SW, Suite 7SW-1009, Mail Stop 3005, Washington, D.C. 20472-3005.
- Contact your state consumer protection office.
- Report corruption, fraud, waste or abuse to the DHS Office of the Inspector General.
- File a complaint with the National Center for Disaster Fraud.

[Relay note: to check whether a call, text or email really came from FEMA, see FEMA's list of official phone numbers and email addresses in "I Applied for Assistance. What's Next?" or call 1-800-621-3362.]
