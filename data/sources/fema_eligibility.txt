Eligibility Criteria for FEMA Assistance
Source: FEMA.gov, https://www.fema.gov/assistance/individual/program/eligibility (last updated June 26, 2026). U.S. government work.

When you apply for assistance, FEMA checks that you meet certain requirements before giving assistance.

Who is Eligible for the Individuals and Households Program?

Citizenship Status: Only United States citizens, non-citizen nationals, or qualified aliens are eligible to receive assistance from FEMA, so FEMA verifies applicants' status before providing assistance. [Relay note: a household can still qualify through a minor child who is a U.S. citizen or qualified alien; see FEMA's citizenship and immigration status page.]

Identity Verification: You need to prove your identity with a valid Social Security number. FEMA usually checks your identity using public records when you apply. If FEMA cannot verify your identity this way, it might ask for more information.

Ownership/Occupancy Verification: For certain types of assistance, FEMA must confirm the disaster-damaged home is your primary residence. For Home Repair or Replacement Assistance, FEMA also needs to confirm you owned the residence at the time of the disaster. FEMA usually verifies this through an automated public records search when you apply. If that search cannot confirm it, FEMA might ask you for documents.

Unmet Need After Insurance: FEMA cannot provide assistance for needs already met by another source, like insurance or other programs. But if your insurance or another program does not cover all your disaster-caused needs, you may be eligible. You must tell FEMA about any insurance coverage you have. If you have insurance, you will need to give FEMA proof of the insurance settlement or a letter explaining you were denied coverage before FEMA can decide what assistance you are eligible for.

Documents can be uploaded to your DisasterAssistance.gov account, or you can visit a document drop-off center near you.

How to Apply

If you think you meet these criteria, you can apply for FEMA Individuals and Households Program assistance at DisasterAssistance.gov. You can also apply in person, online, or by phone (FEMA Helpline 1-800-621-3362).

Legal Requirements

FEMA is required by law to make sure assistance is given correctly, is not duplicated by other sources, is used for necessary disaster-related expenses, and is not obtained through fraud. When you apply, you must say that all the information you provide is true. Your application is a legal document, signed under penalty of perjury: knowingly giving false information can lead to criminal charges, including up to five years in prison. FEMA may check your information against other sources and will try to collect any assistance obtained through fraud.
