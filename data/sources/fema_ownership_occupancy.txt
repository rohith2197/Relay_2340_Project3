Verifying Home Ownership or Occupancy
Source: FEMA.gov, https://www.fema.gov/assistance/individual/after-applying/verifying-home-ownership-occupancy (last updated June 26, 2026). U.S. government work.

To be eligible for certain types of disaster assistance, FEMA must verify you lived at the address in your application as your primary residence. If you are a homeowner, FEMA must also verify you own your home before providing home repair or replacement assistance.

FEMA usually verifies ownership and occupancy through an automated public records search when you apply. If that search cannot verify it, you will be asked to provide documents. FEMA understands that providing documents after a disaster can be hard, so it accepts many types of documents. The fastest way to send them is to upload them at DisasterAssistance.gov.

Proof of Occupancy (you only need ONE of these):
- Lease or housing agreement
- Rent receipts
- Utility bill (electric, water/sewer, etc.)
- Employer's statement (e.g. pay stub)
- Bank or credit card statement
- Driver's license, state-issued ID card, or voter registration card
- Public official's document (Police Chief, Mayor, Postmaster, etc.)
- Medical provider's bill
- Social service organization documents (e.g. Meals on Wheels)
- Motor vehicle registration
- Affidavits of Residency or other court documentation
- Local school documents (must include the child's disaster-damaged residence and the name of the applicant or co-applicant)
- Letter written after the disaster by a mobile home park owner or manager confirming you lived there at the time

Most documents can be dated within 1 year before the disaster and/or within the 18-month period of assistance. A driver's license, state ID or voter registration card must be dated before the disaster and must not be expired when you send it.

Proof of Ownership (you only need ONE of these):
- Deed or official record
- Mortgage documentation
- Homeowners insurance documentation
- Property tax receipt or bill
- Manufactured home certificate or title
- Home purchase contracts (e.g. Bill of Sale, Bond for Title, Land Installment Contract)
- Will or affidavit of heirship (with death certificate) naming you heir to the property
- Receipts for major repairs or maintenance dated within 5 years before the disaster
- Court documents
- Letter written after the disaster by a mobile home park owner or manager or a public official that meets FEMA requirements

Most documents can be dated within 1 year before the disaster or within the 18-month period of assistance.

If You Cannot Get These Documents (last resort)

FEMA allows a written self-declarative statement as a last resort in specific situations:

- Occupancy: if you lived in a mobile home or travel trailer, or on an island, insular area or tribal land, and have none of the accepted documents.
- Ownership: if you own a home on an island, insular area or tribal land, or in a travel trailer or mobile home, and have none of the accepted documents. This also applies if you own and live in a home passed down through heirship (include a copy of the death certificate).

The statement must include the address of the damaged home, how long you lived there as your primary residence before the disaster declaration, your (or your co-applicant's) name and signature, an explanation of why you could not get the usual documents after a good-faith effort with FEMA, and a declaration under penalty of perjury that it is true and correct. For ownership, it must also state that you are the legal owner, or pay no rent but are responsible for taxes or maintenance, or hold lifetime occupancy rights. For inherited homes, it must name the person who died and the date of death, and state that you are the nearest relative in the line of succession.
