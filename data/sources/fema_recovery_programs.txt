Programs to Support Your Recovery: free legal help, case management, crisis counseling
Source: FEMA.gov, https://www.fema.gov/assistance/individual/disaster-survivors (last updated August 17, 2026). U.S. government work.

FEMA has many services to help you in addition to financial assistance.

Disaster Legal Services (free legal help)

Disaster Legal Services (DLS) provides legal help to survivors of a presidentially declared major disaster. These services are for survivors who qualify as low-income and are limited to cases that would not normally involve legal fees. Typical help includes:
- Insurance claims for medical bills, loss of property and loss of life
- New wills, powers of attorney and other legal papers lost in the disaster
- Home repair contracts and disputes with contractors and/or landlords
- Proof of home ownership
- FEMA appeals

DLS attorneys are not FEMA employees. Services are provided in partnership with the American Bar Association Young Lawyers Division. They do not share information with FEMA. To find services in your area, visit the ABA Young Lawyers Division disaster legal services page.

Crisis Counseling and Mental Health

Free and confidential crisis counseling may be offered in your area to help you manage stress, process trauma, cope with losses, and connect with other resources. Counselors can visit you at home, at a shelter, your temporary home or a place of worship at no cost. The program does not provide full mental health or psychiatric services. For free, confidential crisis support in any language, call or text the Disaster Distress Helpline at 800-985-5990.

Disaster Unemployment Assistance

If you could not work as a direct result of a presidentially declared major disaster, you may be eligible for Disaster Unemployment Assistance (DUA). You must:
- Have become unemployed (including self-employed) as a direct result of the disaster;
- Be a U.S. citizen, non-citizen national or qualified alien;
- Not qualify for regular state unemployment insurance;
- Have worked, been self-employed, or been scheduled to start work in a declared county; and
- Show that the work you can no longer do was your primary source of income.
DUA is run by the U.S. Department of Labor, funded by FEMA, and administered by the state, tribal or territorial workforce agency.

Disaster Case Management

You may be able to get a case manager to help with your recovery if the program is approved for your disaster. A case manager works with you to make and carry out a long-term recovery plan and can refer you to help with financial, physical, emotional or other unmet needs. Call FEMA's Helpline at 1-800-621-3362 to ask whether case managers are available in your area.

Other Resources

Help may also be available from sources other than FEMA, listed in FEMA's national, state and local referral lists. Ready.gov has tools for finding family and friends, finding open shelters, emergency alerts, coping with disasters and the FEMA mobile app.

Please note: FEMA's Individual Assistance programs are meant to meet only essential needs, not to cover all losses. You may get help from more than one program, but not for the same exact need from more than one program or source, including insurance.
