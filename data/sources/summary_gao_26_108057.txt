GAO-26-108057: FEMA Actions Needed to Better Support People with Disabilities in Disasters (summary)
Summary written by the Relay team of a U.S. Government Accountability Office report published September 30, 2026: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108057.

Barriers: unclear eligibility; hard-to-understand letters and applications.

Main points:
- People with disabilities are more likely than other survivors to face hardship after a major disaster.
- Stakeholders told GAO that FEMA's application can be hard to use: application questions can be unclear, and FEMA's eligibility letters are written at a grade 12 reading level.
- FEMA had not asked people with disabilities for feedback on its overall application process.
- From 2022 through 2025, 4 percentage points fewer applicants with disabilities were approved than applicants without disabilities.
- FEMA does offer some direct help: disaster planning and recovery guidance for people with disabilities and caregivers, grants that can replace assistive devices and medically required equipment, sign language interpretation by video to help people apply, and staff who investigate disability discrimination complaints.
- FEMA removed its disability-inclusion training for state and local partners in 2025, and regional coordination was inconsistent.
- GAO recommended that FEMA bring back the training, standardize the role of regional disability specialists, and ask for feedback on the accessibility of its application. FEMA agreed.

Why it matters: hard-to-read letters and unclear questions are a real barrier. Explaining FEMA letters and next steps in plain language helps survivors, especially people with disabilities and older adults.
