"Doubly vulnerable": families without resources who lost everything in the Valencia DANA floods (summary)
Summary written by the Relay team of a Público article published November 13, 2024: https://www.publico.es/sociedad/vulnerables-partida-doble-familias-recursos-han-perdido-dana.html. This is a summary, not the original text.

Barriers: documents; red tape; fear of government offices; lack of information.

Setting: the October-November 2024 DANA floods in the province of Valencia, Spain.

Main points:
- Bureaucracy and lack of information were the main barriers keeping low-income families from getting aid.
- According to Oxfam Intermón, undocumented migrants, sex workers, homeless people and families renting single rooms found the aid practically out of reach, because they often had no rental contract, no municipal registration (padrón) and no bank account.
- People who lost their identity documents in the flood had to get them reissued before they could claim aid. Local offices could renew national ID cards for citizens but not foreigners' ID numbers, so many migrants had to travel to the city of Valencia to replace them.
- One person interviewed said some aid required giving up paid work.

Why it matters: people who were already vulnerable before the flood faced the most obstacles afterward, mostly around paperwork and proof. Knowing which alternative documents are accepted, and where to replace lost documents, reduces these barriers.
