Undocumented families hesitant to seek FEMA aid after the Texas Hill Country floods (summary)
Summary written by the Relay team of a Spectrum News article published July 2025: https://spectrumlocalnews.com/tx/south-texas-el-paso/news/2025/07/24/undocumented-families-hesitant-to-seek-aid-after-floods. This is a summary, not the original text.

Barrier: fear and distrust; unclear eligibility.

Setting: the July 2025 floods in the Texas Hill Country, reported from the Hunt, Texas area in Kerr County.

Main points:
- Many undocumented families who lost homes or belongings in the floods avoided registering for FEMA aid because they feared being targeted by immigration authorities.
- Many did not know that a household can still qualify through an eligible member. FEMA asks for a Social Security number, and it can belong to any eligible adult or minor child in the household, such as a child born in the United States.
- According to the state emergency management agency, more than 6,000 people had registered for FEMA aid at the time, but community leaders said some families, especially undocumented ones, were still hesitant.
- Local advocates were helping families through their fear and through the application.

Why it matters: fear and missing information, not only the rules, kept eligible households from applying. Clear information about who can apply, how a U.S. citizen child can qualify the household, and how FEMA handles applicant information helps address this. See FEMA's citizenship and immigration status page and FEMA's citizenship FAQ.
