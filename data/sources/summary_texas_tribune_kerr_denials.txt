FEMA has denied or not advanced most Kerr County aid applications after the July 4 flood (summary)
Summary written by the Relay team of a Texas Tribune article by Emily Foxhall, published October 16, 2025 and republished by KERA News. Mirror: https://www.hppr.org/hppr-news/2025-10-17/fema-has-denied-or-not-advanced-most-kerr-county-aid-applications-after-deadly-july-4-flood. This is a summary, not the original text.

Barriers: appeals and next steps; inaccessible communication methods.

Setting: Kerr County, Texas, about three months after the deadly July 4, 2025 flood on the Guadalupe River (FEMA disaster DR-4879-TX).

Main points (figures as of October 11, 2025):
- Kerr County residents had filed 3,228 applications to FEMA's Individuals and Households Program.
- 1,749 applications, more than half, had not moved past the initial stage of review.
- Of the applications FEMA reviewed, 704 were found eligible, about 22%.
- FEMA had denied 775 applications, largely for procedural reasons such as applicants not responding to FEMA or withdrawing their applications.
- For comparison, a Carnegie Endowment for International Peace researcher found that across 170 disasters from 2015 to May 2024, FEMA found about 39% of applicants eligible. Kerr County's rate was the lowest among the counties hit by the July floods.
- FEMA required an email address, which was a barrier for older residents with limited internet access.
- It was not clear whether people who were denied understood why, or knew they could appeal.
- FEMA did not respond to the reporter's requests for comment.

Why it matters: many denials were about paperwork and follow-up, not about whether people were harmed. Knowing what a FEMA letter asks for, responding on time, using phone or in-person options, and knowing a denial can be appealed within 60 days can change the outcome.
