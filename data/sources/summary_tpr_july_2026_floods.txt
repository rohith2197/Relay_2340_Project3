FEMA won't help Texans after the July 2026 floods as agency tells states to lead disaster response (summary)
Summary written by the Relay team of a Texas Tribune article by Emily Foxhall, published October 21, 2026 and republished by Texas Public Radio: https://www.tpr.org/environment/2026-10-21/fema-wont-help-texans-after-july-2026-floods-as-agency-tells-states-to-lead-disaster-response. This is a summary, not the original text.

Barriers: next steps when federal aid is not available.

Setting: floods in Texas in July 2026 affecting Uvalde and Kerr County, one year after the deadly July 2025 Hill Country floods.

Main points:
- FEMA denied the request for federal Individual Assistance for people affected by the July 2026 floods, as the agency pushed states to lead disaster response.
- Governor Greg Abbott and the Texas Division of Emergency Management said they were reviewing the decision and the process for appealing it. The article did not describe an appeal process for individual residents.
- FEMA said it weighs factors such as insurance coverage, other recent disasters, population, unemployment and the state's own financial capacity when deciding on Individual Assistance.
- On October 15, Abbott asked for federal tax deadlines to be suspended and late fees waived for affected residents.
- Local help came from nonprofits: the Hill Country Community Foundation reported giving about $1.3 million to 947 households.

Why it matters: FEMA individual aid is only available when a disaster declaration includes Individual Assistance. When it is not available, survivors depend on state programs, nonprofits, community foundations and voluntary agencies, which help regardless of immigration status.
