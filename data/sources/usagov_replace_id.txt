How to replace lost or stolen ID cards and documents
Source: USAGov, https://www.usa.gov/replace-vital-documents (last updated November 17, 2025). U.S. government work.

Rules for replacing government-issued ID cards vary. Many require you to show an official document, like your birth certificate, to prove your identity or citizenship.

Who to contact for each type of ID:
- Driver's license or state ID card: contact your state motor vehicle agency for a replacement.
- Social Security card: use the Social Security Administration website to replace your card online, by mail, or in person.
- Birth certificate: contact the vital records office of the state or territory where you were born to get a certified copy.
- Medicare card: log in to or create a Medicare account online to print a copy or request a replacement by mail, or call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227; TTY 1-877-486-2048).
- Medicaid card: contact your state Medicaid office.
- U.S. passport: report the lost or stolen passport to the State Department, then request a replacement in person.
- Permanent Resident (Green) Card: contact U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services to replace your Green Card.
- Naturalization certificate: submit Form N-565 to replace a certificate of citizenship or naturalization.
- Military ID card: report the missing card to your unit security officer, then make an appointment at an ID card office.

If you need help, USAGov answers government-related questions for free by phone.

[Relay note, not from USAGov: for a FEMA application, you usually do not need to replace every document first. FEMA first tries to verify identity, occupancy and ownership through public records, and accepts many alternative documents (see FEMA's "Verifying Home Ownership or Occupancy" page). Free disaster legal services can help replace legal papers lost in a disaster.]
